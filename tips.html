06

Best export settings

YouTube, Reels and Shorts. The bitrate, resolution and codec settings that keep quality high and file size low.

  1. YouTube 1080p — H.264 · VBR 2-pass · Target 12 Mbps, max 20 Mbps · Audio AAC 320 kbps.
  2. YouTube 4K — H.264 · Target 45 Mbps, max 60 Mbps · Keyframe interval 2s · Render at maximum depth.
  3. Reels / Shorts — 1080×1920 · H.264 · 10–15 Mbps · Keep under 60s for full quality on Instagram.
  4. Always — Use hardware encoding if the GPU supports it, and match sequence settings to avoid scaling.
07

Motion graphics basics

Easing and timing with the graph editor. The graph editor is most of good motion design.

  1. Never leave linear keyframes — Linear motion looks robotic. Select keys and press F9 (easy ease) as the first habit.
  2. Use the graph editor — Speed graph, not value graph. Shape curves like an S — fast middle, soft start and end.
  3. Add overshoot — Let movement go slightly past the target and settle back.
  4. Anticipation sells action — A tiny move backwards before the main motion makes the animation feel alive.
08

Clean green screen keys

Keylight, despill and edge work. A composite with no green fringe, no halos and no clipped hair.

  1. Light the screen, not the subject — Even green makes an easy key. Fix lighting before blaming the plugin.
  2. Keylight: screen colour and screen matte — Pick the green, raise clip black until the background is pure, then clip white for a solid subject.
  3. Kill the spill — Use Keylight screen despill or a hue shift on the green range.
  4. Refine the matte — Shrink or soften the matte 0.5–1px and use Simple Choker for hair edges.
09

Audio mixing tricks

Essential Sound panel. Dialogue, music and SFX without an external DAW.

  1. Tag your audio — Mark clips as Dialogue, Music or SFX so Premiere shows the right tools.
  2. Auto-duck music — Enable ducking on music so it dips under dialogue. -12 dB, 200 ms fade.
  3. Layer ambience — Room tone under every scene. Keep it -30 dB or lower.
  4. Loudness targets — Dialogue around -6 dB peaks. Export at -14 LUFS for YouTube.
10

Speed ramps and time remapping

Smooth ramps, freeze frames and slow motion, done so the speed change looks intentional.

  1. Enable time remapping — Right-click the clip, Show Clip Keyframes, Time Remapping, Speed in Premiere.
  2. Split the ramp — Add speed keyframes and drag the middle section. The ramp handles ease.
  3. Optical flow for slow motion — Time interpolation: Optical Flow. Twixtor for extreme slow motion.
  4. Freeze frame — Freeze at the peak, add scale +1% over 5 frames, then release into the ramp.
11

Render and preview faster

Cache, proxies and GPU acceleration so After Effects and Premiere stay usable on a mid-range PC.

  1. Move media cache to an SSD — Preferences, Media Cache, fastest drive. Clear old cache monthly.
  2. Use proxies — Right-click footage, Proxy, Create Proxies. Edit smooth, export full quality.
  3. Drop playback resolution — Preview at 1/2 or 1/4. The export is still full resolution.
  4. GPU acceleration on — Mercury Playback Engine (GPU) in Premiere, and hardware-accelerated decode in After Effects.

Practice each step on a small project first. Muscle memory beats theory.
